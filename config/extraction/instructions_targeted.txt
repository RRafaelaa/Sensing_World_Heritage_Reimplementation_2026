6. "heritage_values_discussed" (array)
   • Identify which heritage values are implicitly discussed by the authors based on their textual descriptions and the definitions provided below (this is the ONLY field where limited interpretation is allowed).
   • Include both the primary values and the secondary values in your answer.
   • Record the summarised thinking process (no more than 50 words) for each value you identified.
   • The list of values are below in a hierarchical format with definitions behind：

        SOCIAL VALUES

        • Spiritual – Beliefs, myths, religions (organized or not), legends, stories, and testimonials of past generations.
        • Emotional (individual) – Memory and personal life experiences.
        • Emotional (collective) – Cultural identity, motivation and pride, sense of “place attachment,” and communal value.
        • Allegorical – Objects/places representative of social hierarchy or status.

        ECONOMIC VALUES

        • Use – The function and utility of the asset, original or attributed.
        • Non-use – The asset’s expired function whose value lies in its continued existence (materials), option value (potential future use), and bequest value.
        • Entertainment – The role the asset plays in the contemporary market, especially tourism.
        • Allegorical – Value oriented toward publicizing financial property.

        POLITICAL VALUES

        • Educational – The educational role of heritage assets, often used for political purposes (e.g., nation-building myths, glorification of leaders).
        • Management – The incorporation of the asset into past or present political strategies and policies.
        • Entertainment – Used in strategies for disseminating cultural awareness for political purposes.
        • Symbolic – Emblematic expressions of power, authority, or prosperity deriving from the asset.

        HISTORIC VALUES

        • Educational – Potential of the asset to provide knowledge about the past in the future.
        • Historic-Artistic – Being a rare or unique example of historical, stylistic, or artistic movements now considered historical.
        • Historic-Conceptual – Rare or unique examples retaining conceptual indicators (e.g., architecture, urban planning).
        • Symbolic – Association with important past events.
        • Archaeological – Connected to ancient civilizations.

        AESTHETICAL VALUES

        • Artistic – Original product of creativity and imagination.
        • Notable – Created by a recognized or signature-holding artist or designer.
        • Conceptual – Materialization of conceptual intentions based on a conceptual background.
        • Evidential – Authentic example from a specific decade or part of art/architectural history.

        SCIENTIFIC VALUES

        • Workmanship – Original result of human labor and craftsmanship.
        • Technological – Outstanding skill in techniques or materials.
        • Conceptual – Integral materialization of conceptual intentions (concept-driven design).

        AGE VALUES

        • Workmanship – Craftsmanship value linked to the production period.
        • Existential – Represents memory and the lives of past generations.
        • Maturity – Marks of time (patina) visible on forms, components, and materials.

        ECOLOGICAL VALUES

        • Spiritual – Harmony between the building and its natural/artificial environment.
        • Essential – Presence of ecological ideologies in the design and construction.
        • Existential – Manufactured resources that can be reused, reprocessed, or recycled.
